The starting point

Loujane's idea: one place to run a property portfolio, with an assistant that takes action

"A combination of a property manager, accountant, maintenance coordinator and tenant-service application… many landlords still manage through WhatsApp, Excel sheets, phone calls and separate service providers."Loujane's notes

The basic layer

  • Occupancy across properties
  • Who paid, what's outstanding
  • Renewals coming up
  • Maintenance requests
  • Income, expenses, profit

The "smart" layer

  • Book and compare maintenance quotes
  • Find tenants, market vacancies
  • Track values and rent trends
  • AI advice: renovate, sell, raise rent
  • Many cities and countries

Her sharpest line

"The key would be identifying a customer segment whose needs existing platforms don't adequately serve, rather than competing feature-for-feature."

We agree. The rest of this deck tests every other line in her notes against that one.

Honest first read

The problem is real. But as written, the notes describe five companies.

Strong

  • The pain is real. WhatsApp, Excel and a relative on the ground is how it works today. Late rent goes unchased, renewals get missed, nothing is written down. Data
  • The founder is the user. Loujane has long-term flats, furnished flats and a shift-rented farm across 2 countries and 2 currencies. Data
  • The owner's point of view is underserved. Regional tools serve brokers, property managers or government processes. Opinion
  • Owning across borders is a real gap. We found no tool combining Jordan, Bahrain, Ejar and multiple currencies. Data

Risky

  • Five products in one: property management, accounting, a vendor marketplace, a listings portal and an investment advisor. Some rivals are well funded (Gathern ≈ $266M valuation). Data
  • "AI says sell" has no data to run on. Bahrain has no official price index, and Jordan's public index dates from 2018. Data
  • Vendor marketplace = cold start ×4 cities. Munjz tried a consumer handyman app and pivoted to B2B. Data
  • Four countries at once, each with different laws, payment systems, currencies and dialects. Opinion
  • The Jeddah investor persona is invented. Loujane's real portfolio is a better guide. Opinion

The Jordanian farm, explained

A farm sells blocks of time, not nights

    • The guest group gets exclusive use of the farm: pool, BBQ, garden, Arabic seating hall ديوان. Data
    • Two shifts a day, plus a cheaper 24h bundle. The night shift crosses midnight. Data
    • Cleaning time between shifts is only 0–2 hours, so the caretaker's turnover is the real job. Data
    • Booking a 24h slot blocks both shifts. Times differ for each farm, sometimes for each weekday. Data

    Market: ~5,000 farms in Jordan, ~2,500 rented out; most owners have 1–3. Summer 2026 rents fell ~25% to 100–120 JOD. Data

    What the market teaches us

    Ten lessons, applied to us

    • Work alongside the government systems (Ejar, Ejari, Bahrain registry) instead of replacing them. Store the registration numbers and import their data.
    • The money is in payments, not subscriptions. Buildium's transfer fees, Stessa's banking, upfront rent from Rize and Keyper.
    • Don't charge tenants to fund a free product. TurboTenant and TenantCloud got backlash for it.
    • Small owners get abandoned. DoorLoop added $199+ minimums and Guesty shut its small-host product. Promise to stay with 1–30 units.
    • Keep pricing predictable: flat tiers, free for 1–3 units.
    • Rules limit "smart" advice: the Riyadh freeze, Abu Dhabi's 0% cap, Bahrain's 5% every 2 years.
    • Arabic, Hijri and WhatsApp are the minimum in 2026, not a differentiator.
    • Short stays don't need a channel manager. They need one calendar, deposits and the caretaker's turnover.
    • Don't build a listings portal. Feed listings into the existing ones, with the required ad licences.
    • Trust is the product: proof of payment, payout reliability, deposit fairness. OpenSooq itself tells guests not to pay in advance.

    Put together, this argues for an owner's operating tool for mixed portfolios across borders: one ledger for leases and shifts, built around WhatsApp and the person on the ground. It also argues against building a marketplace or an AI advisor first. Opinion

    Rules and money, by country

    Four countries, four rulebooks. Rules must be settings, not code.

    Leases (Law 27/2014)

    • Register within 1 month on bahrain.bh; BD 5, paid by the landlord Data
    • An unregistered lease can't go to the Rent Disputes Committee; electricity and water (EWA) won't connect without one Data
    • Deposit ≤ 1 month; advance rent ≤ 3 months Data
    • Increase: none within 2 years, then max 5% (residential) with 3 months' notice Data
    • Eviction after 2 consecutive months unpaid Data

    Short stays & tax

    • Furnished flats let for ≤1 month fall outside the rent law; they count as tourism Data
    • No published "holiday home" permit; a grey area Assumption
    • VAT 10%: residential leases exempt Data
    • ~10% municipal tax when the occupant is an expat, charged on the EWA bill Estimate

    Money & data

    • Fawri+, BenefitPay, Fawateer; reachable via Tap Data
    • Data-protection law has an 83-country adequacy list (incl. EU/US/KSA/UAE/Jordan), so cloud hosting is fine Data
    • A RERA licence is needed only if we ever manage property for others Data

    Leases

    • No national e-registry. Written lease plus stamp duty (rate for leases unconfirmed) Data
    • A signed lease is directly enforceable for unpaid rent at the Execution Department Data
    • Eviction needs a notarial notice إنذار عدلي and a 15-day clock Data

    The farm: licensing is murky

    • 2019 guest-house rules gave only preliminary approvals; 2021: detention for unlicensed farms Data
    • 2022: moved to municipalities; Civil Defence plus Health approval if there is a pool Data
    • Bylaw 50/2025 replaced licences with "approval"; whether farms are covered is unclear Assumption
    • Aug 2026: the sector calls itself "effectively unregulated" Data
    • Action: ask a Jordanian lawyer

    Money, tax & data

    • CliQ: 84M transactions in 2024; request-to-pay and refunds built in Data
    • eFAWATEERcom for recurring "bills"; PayTabs, HyperPay, MEPS Data

    Leases (Ejar)

    • The Ejar lease is an executory instrument سند تنفيذي: straight to the Enforcement Court Data
    • Residential rent counts as paid only through Ejar's SADAD or mada (since Jan 2024) Data

    Implications

    Five things the product must do because of the rules

    01

    Never hold the money

    Payment links that pay out straight to the owner, plus "mark as paid + attach proof". No pooled accounts, which need a licence in every country.

    02

    Keep evidence

    Timestamped leases, receipts, notices and photos. Disputes in all four countries turn on registration and proof.

    03

    Rules as settings

    Rent caps, notice periods, deposit limits and freezes per jurisdiction, editable without code. 2026 alone brought three emergency changes.

    04

    Two rental modes

    Tenancy (rent law applies) vs stay (tourism rules apply). Licence fields are optional but tracked.

    05

    Money done right

    3-decimal currencies, USD pegs, Hijri dates shown, Fri–Sat weekends (Sat–Sun in UAE).

    Why Bahrain + Jordan first is lucky: neither has an Ejar-style system that already covers rent collection, and Bahrain's rules are clearly written. The KSA and UAE long-term markets are both more regulated and more crowded. Opinion

    Options, not answers

    Five directions we could take

    For owners of 1–6 units "back home" who live elsewhere, such as Jordanians in the Gulf, or Gulf nationals with property abroad. Shows rent status, renewals, receipts and profit in their currency, with WhatsApp nudges in Arabic sent on their behalf.

    • For: a clear gap; Gulf salaries pay for it; we only track money, never hold it.
    • Against: few events a year, so habits and retention are hard; still needs someone on the ground; a good spreadsheet can copy it.

    Risk: medium-high (retention)

    Year 1: 30 owners in a free beta from Jordanian community groups in Riyadh, Dubai and Doha; paid tier by month 6; target 150 paying.

    A shift calendar, booking link, CliQ deposit tracking, caretaker checklists and damage-deposit settlement for Jordanian farms, then Saudi istirahas and Bahraini chalets.

    • For: weekly use in season, so it becomes a habit; acute pain (double-booking, lost deposits); no owner-side tool exists; Loujane is an insider.
    • Against: ~2,500 rented farms caps the Jordan market; many owners are informal and cash-based; Gathern could add owner tools in KSA.

    Risk: medium (market size)

    Year 1: concierge service for Loujane's farm + 10 others; 50 paying farms by month 9; then test 10 istiraha owners and Bahrain chalets.

    Built around the caretaker ناطور, cleaner or relative who actually turns the key. Voice notes become tasks; photos and timestamps become proof for the owner.

    • For: this is where trust breaks today; fits A and B; voice-to-task works well in 2026.
    • Against: low-end phones, resistance to being monitored; more a feature than a business.

    Verdict: a module, not a brand

    Build it inside whichever direction wins.

    Build only for Loujane. No pricing, no landing page, no other users until it has run her whole portfolio for 2–3 months.

    • For: nearly zero cost, maximum learning, no pressure on family life.
    • Against: fits one owner too closely; can drift into a hobby.

    Verdict: phase 0 of every option

    8–12 weeks, with a clear "graduate or stop" moment.

    Loujane, or a coordinator we hire, manages other remote owners' flats and farms for a fee (5–8% of rent; 15–20% of farm bookings). The software is our internal tool.

    • For: owners pay for results, not dashboards; revenue comes in fast; the software gets tested hard.
    • Against: a people business; needs a property-manager licence in Bahrain; competes with day jobs.

    Combining the research

    Recommendation: one engine for Loujane's mixed portfolio; the farm module goes to market first

    Step 1 · Loujane's portfolio

    Option D · weeks 0–12

    One shared ledger runs her Bahrain flats, Jordan flats and the farm. Success test: she stops opening Excel, and the farm has zero double-bookings.

    Step 2 · Farm owners in Jordan

    Option B (+ C) · months 3–9

    The sharpest, most frequent pain, with no owner-side tool. Loujane can sell it in person. Charge from day one.

    Step 3 · Owners managing from abroad

    Option A (+ E later) · months 9–18

    The same ledger, reminders and caretaker module, aimed at Jordanians in the Gulf. Start only if step 2 passes.

    Not now (written down on purpose)

    Vendor marketplace · tenant-finding portal · "sell / renovate" AI · long-term rentals in KSA or UAE · holding money · native apps · full accounting.

    Where the research disagreed

    The competitor research ranks owners abroad as the biggest gap. The strategist ranks farm shifts first because people feel that pain every week. We chose to sequence them because they share one engine. This is the first decision for you to make. Opinion

    AI, honestly

    "Smart" should mean less typing, not predictions

    Build: realistic in 2026

    Voice note in Arabic → maintenance ticketCaretakers and tenants already send voice notes
    Receipt or CliQ screenshot → expense or paymentThe most frequent typing; watch for 3-decimal amounts
    Contract photo → lease recordGets an owner set up in minutes
    Match transfers to rent dueSuggests a match; a person confirms
    WhatsApp booking and FAQ assistantAllowed if task-specific (Meta banned general chatbots from Jan 2026)
    Monthly report written in plain languageArabic or English, to share with family
    Farm price suggestionsSimple rules first ("Eid night +40%"); learn from data after 1–2 seasons

    Avoid or postpone: no data behind it

    "Should I sell?" / valuationsNo price data in Bahrain; Jordan's public index dates from 2018
    "Renovate for +X% return"No comparable outcomes; a calculator at most
    Predictive maintenanceNo sensors; 5–15 units is too few. Use scheduled maintenance: AC before summer, tank cleaning, pool
    Tenant risk scoresNo credit bureau access; discrimination risk
    AI negotiating or confirming bookings by itselfTrust and legal risk; AI drafts, a person confirms

    "Raise rent?" becomes a rules check instead: is it allowed, how much, and what notice is required. Useful, and correct.

    Principles

    • Lease and Booking are two kinds of agreement that feed one ledger
    • Money stored as integer fils or halalas, with exchange rate per entry
    • Every number links back to a document
    • Rules per jurisdiction stored as data
    • The caretaker is a Party with a role, not a user account

    Phases, with gates

    A roadmap where each phase has to earn the next

    Phase 0 · Loujane (8–12 wks)

    • Properties, units, farm shifts; BHD + JOD
    • Leases + rent schedule; farm bookings with deposits
    • Ledger, screenshot and receipt capture, profit by unit and country
    • WhatsApp reminders + caretaker inbox
    • Work orders + quote comparison; inspections with photos
    • Monthly report; import from Excel

    Gate: Loujane uses it weekly for 8 weeks, makes zero double-bookings, and gets her monthly profit in under 10 minutes.

    Phase 1 · Private beta (10–20 owners)

    • Multiple accounts; roles: owner, co-owner, caretaker, accountant
    • Public booking page + CliQ / BenefitPay payment links
    • WhatsApp assistant (owner approves)
    • Bank-statement matching; cheque register
    • Rule packs per country
    • Farm pricing rules; subscription billing

    Gate: ≥70% of owners active weekly at week 8 and ≥40% convert to paid.

    Phase 2 · Services (12+ months)

    • Vetted vendor network (commission)
    • Payment collection with fees
    • Premium services: inspections, registration help, notices
    • Anonymised rent comparisons for Bahrain and Jordan (where official data is missing)
    • Calendar sync with Gathern and Airbnb

    Gate: revenue per owner, ≥15% of owners buy a service, dispute rate <3%.

    Loujane's original vision (marketplace, comparing quotes, market insight) doesn't disappear. It moves to phase 2, after we've earned the data and the trust.

    Research gate (startup-design method)

    Yellow light: go ahead with interviews, don't build a product yet

    Strongest signals

    • No product handles mixed leases and shifts in one ledger
    • No landlord tool exists in Jordan; CliQ is everywhere
    • Farm owners' pains (double-booking, deposits, damage) are concrete and frequent
    • Loujane is the user, and an insider in the farm world

    Red flags

    • Small market: ~2,500 rented farms in Jordan
    • Willingness to pay is unproven, and small landlords pay little
    • BaiTech already targets Bahrain landlords at 1 BHD per unit per month
    • Farm licensing in Jordan is unclear

    What must be true

    • Farm owners lose real money to the problems we solve, and say so unprompted
    • They'll pay roughly a booking's worth of profit per month
    • Caretakers will send photos and voice notes
    • Loujane keeps using it after the novelty fades

    How to interview without fooling yourself

    • Don't mention the app. Say: "I'm trying to understand how farm owners handle bookings."
    • Ask about the last time: "Tell me about your last double-booking / deposit fight."
    • Ask what they already pay for or have built (brokers, ads, a notebook system). That's real signal.
    • Never ask "would you use…?" Everyone says yes to be polite.
    • "Interesting!" means nothing. Money spent and time lost mean something.
    • End with: "Who else should I talk to?"
